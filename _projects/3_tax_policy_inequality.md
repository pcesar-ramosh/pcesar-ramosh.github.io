**Paper:** Monzón, C., Ramos, C. and Romero, I. (2025). *Growth-Inequality Trade-off in Tax Policy: A Heterogeneous Agents Approach.* [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5139413)

The study analyzes how changes in labor and consumption taxes affect macroeconomic variables and income distribution in Bolivia, using a heterogeneous-agent overlapping-generations (OLG) model. The model includes age-specific productivity, idiosyncratic uninsured income shocks and permanent differences in ability. It is calibrated with aggregate macroeconomic data, Bolivia's Household and Employment Survey and values from the literature, so that it reproduces the country's income distribution.

**Findings.** Lower tax rates raise capital accumulation, labor supply and growth, mainly benefiting deciles 6 to 9, but they reduce the income share of lower-income groups and raise the Gini coefficient. Tax increases have the opposite effects.
